# PyCircuitSim V6.6.1 — DirectNet Recipe-Comparison Accuracy Report

**Date:** 2026-07-01  **Branch:** `V6.6`  **Model family:** DirectNet (LEVEL=73)

> **Status note (2026-07-03).** Historical record of the single-lever recipe study.
> Its §7 default decision (clean stays default) was superseded by the crit30
> curriculum promotion (V6.6.3/V6.6.4, 14/16 strict), and the study now extends
> across capacity tiers (V6.6.5 size matrix; V6.6.6 xl curriculum wave). The
> consolidated conclusions + current production recommendation live in
> `docs/V6.6.6-accuracy-report.md`. csob's standing as the AC/device-fidelity
> alternate (§6) is unchanged.

> **Question this report answers.** V6.6.0 shipped the *uniform clean recipe*
> (`--apply-filter off --swa-mode ema --seed 42`, no loss preset / EKV / Sobolev /
> corridor / T3) and reported its honest fidelity: **13/16 complex gates at the
> production `large` tier**. This report asks the natural follow-up — **is there a
> *different uniform* training recipe that does better?** It sweeps a family of
> uniform recipes (charge-Sobolev, Sobolev, EKV core, a uniform-seed sweep, and two
> combos) across **all four capacity scales** and four techs, gated against the
> identical NGSPICE BSIM-CMG (LEVEL=72) ground truth, and picks the best as the new
> default.
>
> "Uniform" is load-bearing: every recipe is **one identical addendum applied to
> every (tech × device × size) checkpoint** — never a per-tech / per-gate special
> (those were retired in V6.6.0). The clean V6.6.0 recipe is the control.

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **No uniform recipe, seed, or combo beats the clean control's 13/16 at the
  production `large` tier.** Full `large` scoreboard: **clean 13** > csob 12 >
  cs7 / s7 / s17 11 > csobekv / ekv / s123 10 > sob 5.
- The complex-gate ceiling is set by a **mutual exclusivity of the value-surface
  basins** (opamp high-gain, ring switching-edge, and — for the EKV recipes — SRAM
  butterfly): each recipe/seed lands a *different* subset, and winning one tends to
  lose another. **Seed-42 (the V6.6.0 clean default) lands the single largest
  simultaneously-compatible set.**
- The **combo hypothesis is refuted**: `csobekv` (csob + EKV core) *does* combine two
  basins no single recipe reaches (it is the only recipe to pass tsmc7-ring **and**
  tsmc16-opamp together, and it gets tsmc5-ring to 5.80 %, the closest of any large
  checkpoint to the 5 % gate) — **but the EKV analytic core breaks tsmc12+tsmc16
  SRAM and tsmc12 opamp**, netting only 10/16. Stacking basins is zero-sum here.
- **`csob` (charge-Sobolev) is the strongest all-round alternative**: best device
  NRMSE at `large` (1.50 vs clean 1.70), better AC (5/12 vs 4/12), more total
  gate-passes summed over all scales (41 vs 40), and the only recipe that lands the
  tsmc16 opamp on the clean seed — for the cost of one complex gate at `large`
  (12 vs 13).
- **Recommendation: keep `clean` as the production default** (it is the unique best
  at the production-tier headline metric; no uniform recipe beats it) and promote
  **`csob` as the documented, one-env-var alternative** for AC- / device-fidelity-
  sensitive use. See §7 — the final default is the user's call between "preserve the
  headline (clean)" and "improve the aggregate (csob)".

## 2. Methodology

- **Recipes (uniform addenda on top of the clean base recipe):**
  | tag | addendum | rationale |
  |---|---|---|
  | `clean` | — (control) | V6.6.0 production recipe |
  | `csob` | `--charge-sobolev` | couple autograd ∂q/∂V to the supervised caps (AC / dynamics) |
  | `sob` | `--sobolev --sobolev-corridor-only` | couple autograd ∂id/∂V to OSDI gm/gds/gmb (conducting corridor) |
  | `ekv` | `--ekv-core` | analytic EKV/charge-sheet backbone + bounded NN residual |
  | `s7` / `s17` / `s123` | `--seed {7,17,123}` | **uniform** global seed change (basin probe) |
  | `csobekv` | `--charge-sobolev --ekv-core` | **combo** — cap-consistency + EKV backbone |
  | `cs7` | `--charge-sobolev --seed 7` | **combo** — best loss term + alternate basin |
- **Matrix:** recipe × {small, medium, large, xl} × {tsmc5, tsmc7, tsmc12, tsmc16} ×
  {NMOS, PMOS}. Capacity tiers: small 128×3, medium 256×5, large 384×6, xl 512×8.
  (The seed sweep + combos are concentrated at `large` — the decision-relevant tier;
  the four core recipes cover all four scales.)
- **Ground truth:** **always** NGSPICE on the identical BSIM-CMG (LEVEL=72) OSDI
  model (repo `tools/ngspice-45.2`). CPU-pinned (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""
  OMP=MKL=1`).
- **Gates (per circuit, 4 circuits × 4 techs = 16):** ring period-err ≤ 5 %; opamp
  open-loop DC-gain-err ≤ 10 % (trip-shift reported, not gated); switchcap
  charge-err ≤ 5 % + droop; SRAM butterfly all-lobes-positive. Plus device Id-Vgs
  sweeps (NRMSE), inverter VTC/transient, AC CS-amp (gain0/f3db/magNRMSE), opamp AC.
- **Checkpoints** saved as `tsmc{X}_dn_{recipe}_{size}_{dev}` — the clean production
  `tsmc{X}_dn_{size}_{dev}` checkpoints are the control and are **never** clobbered.
- **Reproduce:** `scripts/recipe_train.sh` → `scripts/recipe_eval.sh` →
  `scripts/recipe_collect.py` (full auto-generated tables in
  `results/recipe_bench/ACCURACY_REPORT.md`, MATRIX section — formerly RECIPE_REPORT.md).

- **`large` (production tier): clean 13 is the best single cell.** csob 12, ekv 10,

- **Summed over all four scales, `csob` (41) edges out clean (40)** — it is better
  at `small` (+2) and ties at `medium`/`xl`, paying back the single-gate `large`
  deficit elsewhere.

## 4. The complex-gate ceiling: mutually-exclusive opamp basins

The headline `large` matrix for the *value-surface-fragile* gates (opamp, ring) —
the gates that decide the 13-vs-better question — across all recipes:

| Tech · gate | clean | csob | ekv | s7 | s17 | s123 |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| tsmc5 ring | F 12.7% | F 10.3% | F 6.1% | F 12.6% | F 9.6% | F 11.8% |
| tsmc7 ring | **P 4.8%** | F 5.1% | F 6.7% | F 7.2% | F 8.7% | F 7.2% |
| tsmc12 ring | P | P | P | P | P | P |
| tsmc16 ring | P | P | P | P | P | P |
| tsmc5 opamp | **P 2.1%** | F | **P 1.6%** | **P 0.2%** | F | F |
| tsmc7 opamp | F | F | F | F | F | F |
| tsmc12 opamp | **P 6.3%** | **P 5.8%** | **P 7.0%** | F | F | F |
| tsmc16 opamp | F | **P 1.3%** | F | F | **P 6.2%** | F |

**The opamp gates are mutually exclusive across recipe×seed.** Each recipe lands
exactly **two** of the four opamp gates, but a *different* two:

| recipe | opamp gates landed |
|---|---|
| clean (s42), ekv | {tsmc5, tsmc12} |
| csob | {tsmc12, tsmc16} |
| s7 | {tsmc5} (seed-7 *breaks* tsmc12!) |
| s17 | {tsmc16} (seed-17 *breaks* tsmc5 and tsmc12) |

- **tsmc12 opamp passes only with seed-42** — every seed change (s7/s17/s123) breaks
  it. This is why the uniform-seed sweep cannot beat clean: trading seeds to win
  tsmc16 (s17) loses tsmc12 *and* tsmc5.
- **tsmc7 opamp passes nowhere** — it is the gate V6.5.9 needed the per-case T3
  differentiable-solver to close; no uniform recipe reaches it.
- **tsmc5 ring passes nowhere at `large`** (ekv gets closest, 6.1 % vs the 5 %
  gate) — though **ekv @ `medium` is the one configuration in the entire sweep that
  passes tsmc5 ring**, at the cost of other gates (ekv medium = 11/16).
- So the binding constraint on "beat 13" is **simultaneity**: the open gates are
  each individually reachable by *some* uniform recipe/scale, but no single uniform
  recipe lands a *new* one without dropping an old one. The combos (§5) test whether
  stacking the loss term + EKV backbone *structurally* (not via the fragile seed
  axis) can break this.

## 5. Combos — can stacking grab a third basin? (refuted)

Two combos were trained at `large` to test whether stacking the loss term + EKV
backbone *structurally* (not via the fragile seed axis) could break the
mutual-exclusivity ceiling:

| combo | @large | value-surface basins it lands |
|---|:---:|---|
| `csobekv` (csob + `--ekv-core`) | **10/16** | opamp {tsmc16}; ring {tsmc7, tsmc12, tsmc16}; **loses SRAM {tsmc12, tsmc16}** |
| `cs7` (csob + `--seed 7`) | **11/16** | opamp {tsmc5}; ring {tsmc12, tsmc16}; SRAM 4/4 |

**The hypothesis is refuted, but instructively.** `csobekv` genuinely does something
no single recipe does — it is the **only checkpoint in the entire sweep to pass
tsmc7-ring AND tsmc16-opamp simultaneously** (clean gets tsmc7-ring but not
tsmc16-opamp; csob gets the reverse), and its EKV conduction core pushes **tsmc5-ring
to 5.80 %** — the closest any `large` checkpoint gets to the 5 % gate (vs clean's
12.66 %). So the EKV core *does* reshape the ring/opamp value surface favourably.
**But the same EKV analytic core breaks the tsmc12 and tsmc16 SRAM butterfly** (it
regresses the sub-threshold shape the SRAM latch needs — the known EKV SRAM
regression) **and tsmc12 opamp**, so the net is 10/16 — *worse* than clean. `csobekv`
also converged to a validation loss **10–40× worse** than clean (EKV core + charge-
Sobolev fight on the shared `id` head), consistent with its weaker gate result.

`cs7` behaves as the seed analysis predicts: seed-7 lands tsmc5-opamp (0.43 %) but
breaks tsmc12-opamp, so it is csob's profile shifted by one basin — 11/16.

**Conclusion: basin-stacking is zero-sum on this value surface.** Every lever that
opens a new basin (EKV core → tsmc7-ring + tsmc16-opamp; seed → tsmc5-opamp) closes
an equal-or-greater number elsewhere (SRAM, tsmc12-opamp). Reaching 14/16 uniformly
would require a lever that adds a basin *without* perturbing the others — which is
precisely what the retired per-case specials (corridor, T3 differentiable solver)
did per-gate, and what no *uniform* recipe in this sweep achieves.

## 6. Device-level, AC, and aggregate fidelity (where csob earns its keep)

### Device-level mean NRMSE % (lower = better)

| Recipe | small | medium | large | xl | mean |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| clean | 1.63 | **1.29** | 1.70 | 2.17 | 1.70 |
| **csob** | 1.77 | 1.33 | **1.50** | **1.77** | **1.59** |
| sob | 1.92 | 1.38 | 1.95 | 2.22 | 1.87 |
| ekv | 1.99 | 1.72 | 2.38 | 2.18 | 2.07 |

### AC device CS-amp pass-rate (X / 12, per recipe×scale; gain0/f3db/magNRMSE gates)

| Recipe | small | medium | large | xl | Σ |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| clean | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 17 |
| **csob** | **7** | 4 | **5** | **5** | **21** |
| sob | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 18 |
| ekv | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 18 |

**`csob` (charge-Sobolev) is the cleanest all-round win:** it directly supervises the
autograd ∂q/∂V the AC/transient solvers consume, and it shows — **best mean device
NRMSE (1.59 vs clean 1.70), best AC pass-count (21 vs 17), and it is the only recipe
that lands the tsmc16 opamp at `large` on the clean seed.** Its one cost is the
`large` complex-gate count (12 vs 13): it trades {tsmc5 opamp, tsmc7 ring} for
{tsmc16 opamp}. Note this *refutes* the V6.5.x "charge-Sobolev is dead on arrival"
verdict, which was measured only at `medium` — at `large`/`xl` it is a measurable
device+AC improvement.

At `large`, the AC pass-count ordering is **`csobekv` 6/12 > `csob` 5/12 = ekv 5/12 >
clean 4/12**: coupling the autograd dQ/dV to the supervised caps (charge-Sobolev) —
and, further, the EKV analytic core — measurably improves the small-signal cap fit,
the V6.6.0 report's noted weakness. `csobekv` wins AC but loses complex gates and
device NRMSE, so it is not a viable default; `csob` is the AC/device win without the
complex-gate collapse.

## 7. Default decision & recommendation

The sweep answers the question negatively but precisely: **no uniform recipe improves
the production-tier headline metric** (complex gates at `large`), so the honest
outcome is that **the V6.6.0 clean recipe is already optimal there**. The decision is
therefore between two well-characterised policies:

| | **`clean`** (current default) | **`csob`** (charge-Sobolev) |
|---|---|---|
| Complex gates @ `large` (headline) | **13/16** | 12/16 |
| Complex gates Σ all scales | 40 | **41** |
| Device mean NRMSE @ `large` | 1.70 | **1.50** |
| AC device @ `large` | 4/12 | **5/12** |
| tsmc16 opamp @ `large` | fail | **pass** |
| what it loses vs the other | tsmc16 opamp | tsmc5 opamp + tsmc7 ring |

**Recommendation — keep `clean` as the production default.** It is the unique best at
the project's headline metric (13/16 complex gates at the production `large` tier),
and adopting any other uniform recipe would regress it. `csob` improves the
*aggregate* (device NRMSE, AC, small-tier, total gate-passes) and is the right choice
if small-signal / device-fidelity accuracy is weighted above the single large-tier
complex gate — so it is promoted to a **first-class, one-env-var alternative**:

```bash
# Pin the charge-Sobolev recipe (per tech, NMOS+PMOS) at any tier:
export PYCIRCUITSIM_NN_CHECKPOINT_DN_NMOS=tsmc7_dn_csob_large_nmos
export PYCIRCUITSIM_NN_CHECKPOINT_DN_PMOS=tsmc7_dn_csob_large_pmos
```

All recipe checkpoints (`tsmc{X}_dn_{recipe}_{size}_{dev}`) remain on disk; the clean
production checkpoints are untouched. The resolver default is unchanged
(`pycircuitsim/parser.py` `per_tech_preempt`, `large`-first).

**Decision (confirmed):** the production default stays **`clean`** — it is the unique
best at the production-tier headline metric, and no uniform recipe beats it, so the
honest outcome of this sweep is that the V6.6.0 recipe is already optimal there.
`csob` is retained as the documented, one-env-var alternative for AC-/device-fidelity-
weighted use. Switching would be a one-line resolver edit (prepend
`tsmc{X}_dn_csob_large_{dev}` to `per_tech_preempt`), but is not applied, to preserve
the 13/16 headline.

## 8. What this sweep rules in and out (for the next campaign)

- **Ruled out (uniform):** loss presets that reshuffle targets, Sobolev id-slope
  supervision (KILL), EKV analytic core alone or with charge-Sobolev (breaks SRAM),
  and the global-seed axis (basin lottery; seed-42 already optimal). None reach
  14/16 uniformly.
- **Confirmed useful (uniform, non-default):** **charge-Sobolev** measurably improves
  device NRMSE and the small-signal cap fit (AC) — the V6.6.0-flagged weakness — with
  no complex-gate collapse. It is the recommended alternative and a good base for
  future AC-focused work.
- **The remaining path to a *uniform* 14/16+** is not a training recipe: it is a
  lever that opens a value-surface basin (tsmc7-opamp, tsmc5-ring) *without*
  perturbing the others — i.e. the per-case corridor / T3 differentiable-solver
  techniques applied uniformly, or a solver-side continuation that de-fragilises the
  high-gain fixed points. Those are out of scope for a pure training-recipe sweep.
