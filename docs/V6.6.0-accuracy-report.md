# PyCircuitSim V6.6.0 — Accuracy Report

**Date:** 2026-06-29  **Branch:** `V6.6`  **Model family:** DirectNet (LEVEL=73)

> **Status note (2026-07-03).** Historical record of the clean-recipe baseline. The
> production recipe has since moved to crit30 (V6.6.4, 14/16 strict), and the
> consolidated cross-tier conclusions + current production recommendation live in
> `docs/V6.6.6-accuracy-report.md`.

> This report measures the **genuine, uniform fidelity** of the NN-based compact
> model. Every DirectNet checkpoint is trained from scratch on **one identical
> recipe** — there are **no per-case special recipes** (no corridor harvest,
> seed-tuning, EKV core, or differentiable-solver fine-tunes that the V6.5.x line
> used to force individual gates to pass). What follows is what the model actually
> does, un-cherry-picked.

## 2. Methodology

- **One identical recipe for all 32 checkpoints:** `bsimar.cli.train --model direct
  --apply-filter off --swa-mode ema --seed 42` (no loss preset / EKV / Sobolev /
  monotone / corridor / T3). Capacity (`--size`) is the *only* variable.
- **Matrix:** 4 techs (TSMC5/7/12/16) × {NMOS, PMOS} × {small, medium, large, xl}
  = **32 checkpoints**, each a per-tech local-vocab embedding (Rule 16).
- **Sizes:** small 128×3 (~0.06M p) · medium 256×5 (~0.4M p) · large 384×6 (~0.9M p)
  · xl 512×8 (~2.13M p).
- **Data:** the 8 clean per-tech datasets `datasets/{tech}_{nmos,pmos}.npz`
  (full Vth + geometry grid, inverter-trip + reverse-Vds corridor overlays),
  reused as-is — fresh random-weight init ("from scratch").
- **Ground truth:** **always** NGSPICE on the *identical* BSIM-CMG (LEVEL=72) OSDI
  model (repo `tools/ngspice-45.2`). Never a simplified/self-defined reference.
- **Determinism:** all gates CPU-pinned (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" OMP_NUM_THREADS=1
  MKL_NUM_THREADS=1`).
- **Production default:** the parser resolver prefers `large` first (uniform, all
  techs); any size can be pinned via `PYCIRCUITSIM_NN_CHECKPOINT_DN_{NMOS,PMOS}`.

Reproduce: `scripts/benchmark_train_sml.sh` (train) → `scripts/benchmark_run_tests.sh`
(gate) → `scripts/benchmark_collect.py` (`results/benchmark_sml/REPORT.md`).

## 6. AC small-signal (autograd dQ/dV capacitances) — production `large`

The NN's small-signal caps are autograd derivatives of its predicted terminal
charges (cgd=∂qg/∂Vd, …) — gated for the first time against NGSPICE `.ac` on the
identical L72 model.

| Class | Production (large) | Cross-size |
|---|---|---|
| Device CS-amp (gain0 ≤1.5 dB / f3db ratio ∈[0.7,1.43] / mag NRMSE ≤10 %) | **4/12 PASS** | S 5/12, M 4/12, **L 4/12**, XL 4/12 |
| Opamp open-loop (DC-gain ≤3 dB / GBW ∈[0.6,1.67] / PM ≤15°) | **0/12** | 0/12 every size |

**What is right:** the **DC gain** is excellent everywhere — device gain0 error
< 1.5 dB in 24/24 cells (mean 0.86 dB) — so the autograd gm/gds fed to the AC stamp
are accurate. The dominant **cap-driven pole** is faithful for the well-fit cells
(f3db ratio ≈ 1.0).

**What is the genuine limit:** (1) the **Cgd-feedforward RHP zero** (high-frequency
phase lag) is not reproduced — a clean, specific transcapacitance limitation; (2)
some cells under-predict the output cap (tsmc5 NMOS, tsmc12/16 PMOS, f3db ratio
1.1–1.6) so they miss the magnitude gate; (3) the **opamp** AC inherits the DC
value-surface fragility — where the OP is in the good basin (tsmc12) the NN nails
GBW (0.97×) and phase margin (1.3°), confirming the *dynamics* are right and only
the DC-gain *level* is the miss. These are value-surface / feedforward limits, **not
a charge-derivative deficiency** (which would corrupt gain *and* pole everywhere).

## 7. Capacity analysis — why `large`, and why not `xl`

| Size | Complex gates | Device mean NRMSE% | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| small | 7/16 | 1.63 | under-capacity; opamp 0/4, switchcap 1/4 |
| medium | 10/16 | **1.29** | best device fit; opamp 0/4 |
| **large** | **13/16** | 1.70 | **production sweet spot** |
| xl | 10/16 | 2.17 | **over-fit**: worst off-nominal NRMSE; opamp/ring collapse |

XL fits the training distribution ~10× tighter (validation loss ~2e-4 vs medium's
~2e-3) yet **generalizes worst** to off-nominal geometry/VT (tsmc12 PMOS min-R²
0.37) and **loses every high-gain basin `large` won** (tsmc5 opamp, tsmc12 opamp
PASS→FAIL). This is the cleanest confirmation that **capacity is not the bind**:
beyond `large` the model over-fits the value surface and collapses the high-gain
fixed points. **`large` is the production tier; `xl` is the empirical over-fit
boundary.**

## 8. The fidelity frontier (open gates) — honest accounting

Three gates remain open at production `large` under the uniform recipe:

| Open gate | Headline | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **tsmc5 ring-osc** | period err 12.66 % | NMOS conduction under-drive at the steep 0.65 V switching edge |
| **tsmc7 opamp** | gain → 0 | high-gain fixed point unreachable on the value surface (the hardest gate; V6.5.9 needed the T3 differentiable-solver) |
| **tsmc16 opamp** | gain → 0 | same value-surface high-gain-basin fragility |

These are the *same* gaps the retired V6.5.x specials targeted. They are
**value-surface / fixed-point** properties (open-loop gain, oscillation period at a
sharp edge), **not** device-current or charge-derivative fidelity gaps — device DC,
inverter, switchcap, SRAM, and AC-gain are all strong. Closing them faithfully (vs.
by bespoke per-case tuning) is the standing research frontier, tracked in the
CHANGELOG.
