# V6.6 — NN accuracy improvement + XL capacity tier

Date: 2026-06-22 · Branch: `feat/ac-analysis` · Author: campaign notes

## 1. What the V6.4.9 / V6.5 benchmark established (the starting point)

`results/benchmark_sml/REPORT.md` (S/M/L capacity study + V6.5 AC) is unusually
decisive about *where the accuracy actually lives*:

- **Device-level fidelity is already excellent at every capacity.** DC Id-Vgs +
  inverter VTC/transient mean NRMSE < 4 % (most < 2 %); inverter 16/16 PASS at
  all sizes/techs. AC **gain** (autograd gm/gds) err < 1.5 dB on **24/24** cells.
  Capacity barely moves device curves and at the finer nodes `large` slightly
  *overfits* (tsmc12 DC 0.36 % → 1.25 % medium→large). **Device fidelity is NOT
  the bind.**
- **Circuit pass-rate rises monotonically with capacity: 6 → 9 → 12 / 16**
  (S → M → L). The gains come from charge-transfer / timing circuits.
- **Two gaps survive capacity** and were already root-caused by the V6.4.x
  campaigns:
  1. **opamp open-loop gain collapse** (S+M all techs; recovers at L only for
     tsmc5/tsmc12). Owned by the **DC value-surface** (NR lands a low-gain
     basin). V6.4.7/8 exhausted cheap-lever, capacity (S1), derivative-via-loss
     (S10), solver-continuation (S2, the one win) and structural-EKV (S3).
  2. **tsmc5 switchcap ~11.7–12.1 % over-conduction.** Owned by **µA-band loss
     compression**: the asinh output norm uses a per-target geometric-mean scale
     `s_id`, and we measured `s_id ≈ 1.0–2.6e-5 A` for *every* tech. Currents
     below that knee sit in the asinh-**linear** band where `asinh(id/s_id) ≈
     id/s_id`, so a *relative* error at, say, 2 µA contributes almost no loss
     mass. The switchcap charges a 100 fF cap through a transmission gate over a
     1.9 ns window — the pass devices conduct squarely in that compressed µA
     band, so the net over-conducts.
- **AC RHP-zero phase** (Cgd transcapacitance feed-forward) is not reproduced —
  structural, diagnostic-only, not gated.

The report's bottom line, and the V6.4.8 close, name the next campaign
explicitly: **"µA-band loss/normalization de-compression"** for the switchcap;
the opamp/value-surface and AC-phase gaps are *not* reachable by simple levers
("no retraining warranted").

## 2. Ranked improvement suggestions (simple → medium; no complex methods)

| # | Lever | Class | Targets | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **XL capacity tier (512×8, 2.13 M p)** | simple / mechanical | extend the 6→9→12 capacity curve | low (slight device overfit; bounded by EMA + early-stop) |
| 2 | **µA-band loss de-compression** — reuse the existing default-off `SubthresholdIdLoss` retuned from the sub-nA band to the **µA band** (`s2=1e-7, upper=3e-5`, λ screened 0.02/0.05) | simple→medium / config-only | tsmc5 switchcap over-conduction (the one *loss-owned* device gap) | low — additive term, default-off → stock checkpoints byte-identical; A/B-gated before rollout |
| 3 | EMA + early-stop already in recipe; rely on them (not a new weight-decay schedule) to bound XL device overfit | simple | XL overfit | low |
| — | **opamp gain collapse / AC RHP-zero phase** | NOT pursued | value-surface / transcapacitance-feedforward | prior campaigns proved these are not simple-lever-reachable; report says no retrain warranted |

Why #2 is the right "simple" lever and not a normalization-knee hack: capping
`s_id` globally would also re-weight the near-zero / sign-flipping rows and can
disturb the strong-inversion fit, which is exactly why the project built
`SubthresholdIdLoss` as an *additive* term. Retuning its band from
`s2=1e-9, upper=1e-6` (sub-nA, the SRAM target) to `s2=1e-7, upper=3e-5` (µA)
makes the loss decade-balanced (≈ log) from 0.1–30 µA, so the Huber term
penalizes the over-conduction **ratio** directly while leaving > 30 µA strong
inversion to the base MAE. It is a two-flag config change, no model code, and
cannot regress the default-off shipping checkpoints.

**Datasets:** the clean V6.4.9 datasets (full Vth + geometry, on disk) are
current. Both levers are loss/architecture changes, not data changes → **no
regeneration needed.**

## 4. Results

### Lever #2 (µA-band loss de-compression) — KILL

A/B on medium (tsmc5 = target, tsmc12 = no-regress control), λ ∈ {0.02, 0.05},
`SubthresholdIdLoss` retuned to `s2=1e-7, upper=3e-5`:

| arm | tsmc5 switchcap charge_err | tsmc5 DC no-regress |
|---|---|---|
| stock medium | **11.84 %** (lvl 0.3717 V, NG 0.2948 V) FAIL | clean |
| µA λ=0.05 | **12.05 %** (0.3731 V) FAIL — worse | slight regress |
| µA λ=0.02 | **11.69 %** (0.3707 V) FAIL — flat | slight regress (l_16nm base NRMSE 0.84→2.41 %) |
| tsmc12 λ=0.05 | 4.19 → 4.24 % PASS (no regress) | clean |

The charge level moved < 0.2 % of VDD despite the aux term running ≈ ½ the base
MAE and visibly reshaping the DC fit. **The over-charge survives direct µA-band
loss de-compression** — exactly as it survived the V6.4.8-S3 EKV structural
prior. This *refutes* the V6.4.8 roadmap's "loss-compression-owned" hypothesis:
the tsmc5 switchcap over-charge is **not** µA-band-DC-current-owned. It is a
sample-and-hold **charge/transient** behaviour (the cap charges through the
transmission gate; the DC Id surface is already < 1 % accurate in that band).
Fixing it needs a transient/charge-model investigation — a *complex* lever the
user de-scoped. **Lever reverted** (temp `_uA*` checkpoints removed from the
resolver dir; default-off `SubthresholdIdLoss` infra unchanged and recoverable).

### Campaign decision

Recipe unchanged (clean control-v2). Add the **XL tier** (the surviving simple
lever) for all 4 techs × N/P; the existing V6.4.9 clean-recipe S/M/L checkpoints
are retained (retraining them on the identical recipe only reproduces them), and
**all four tiers are re-evaluated together** for the updated report.
